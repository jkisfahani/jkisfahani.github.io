Ethereum Wallet Security Transparency

A structured review of publicly available security evidence for eight Ethereum wallet products. The research evaluates the availability, relevance, scope and verification status of evidence—not the actual security strength of each wallet.

02. Research question and scope

How much relevant, verifiable security information is publicly available for each wallet, and what does that evidence actually cover?

The review considers independent security audits, bug bounty programs, disclosure policies, official security documentation, security features and advisories, as well as component- and protocol-level evidence.

Wallets included: Coinbase Wallet, Ledger, MetaMask, Rabby, Safe, Trust Wallet, Uniswap Wallet and Zerion.

03. Evidence classification and method

Each record is classified by its relevance and scope. This helps prevent an audit of a protocol, organization or individual component from being presented as an audit of an entire wallet.

  • Direct wallet evidence
  • Component-level evidence
  • Ecosystem-level evidence
  • Privacy-only or protocol-only evidence
  • Audit indexes requiring report-level inspection
  • Unverified product coverage or wallet relevance

Records were counted as verified when their verification status began with “Verified”. The resulting verification rate describes the collected records only; it is not a security score.

04. Selected findings

  • Trust Wallet has the highest number of direct wallet evidence records in this dataset: four.
  • Rabby has three individual independent security audit reports.
  • Coinbase Wallet has the largest evidence collection: six records.
  • Safe has an equal number of direct and component-level evidence records.
  • Uniswap Wallet requires additional wallet-specific evidence verification.
  • Zerion’s current evidence collection contains one record.

These findings describe the collected evidence and should not be interpreted as a comparison of actual wallet safety.

06. Limitations and next steps

  • Evidence counts are unequal across wallets.
  • Audit scopes and technical components differ.
  • Some security and bug bounty information may become outdated.
  • Some audit findings may remain unresolved.
  • Evidence collection is incomplete for some wallets.

Planned improvements include expanding evidence for Zerion, investigating Uniswap Wallet-specific coverage, reviewing additional audit reports, tracking remediation, and adding Frame and Rainbow to a future research phase.

Important interpretation

This is an independent evidence review, not a wallet security certification, vulnerability assessment or ranking of wallet safety. The presence of documentation or an audit does not guarantee that a wallet is secure or free from vulnerabilities.
